Kernel driver ina4230
=====================

Supported chips:

  * Texas Instruments INA4230

    Prefix: 'ina4230'

    Addresses: I2C 0x40 - 0x4f

    Datasheet: Publicly available at the Texas Instruments website

	       https://www.ti.com/product/INA4230

Author: Alexey Charkov <alchark@flipper.net>

Description
-----------

The Texas Instruments INA4230 monitors shunt voltage and bus voltage on up to
four channels. It calculates current, power and accumulated energy for each
channel in hardware, scaled by a per-channel calibration value.

The driver derives the calibration from the shunt resistance and the maximum
expected current of each channel, as described in
Documentation/devicetree/bindings/hwmon/ti,ina4230.yaml. The current LSB is
the maximum expected current divided by 2^15, and the 4x shunt gain is enabled
on channels where that current drops no more than 20.48 mV across the shunt.

By default, all connected channels are enabled, with 4 samples averaged and a
1.1 ms conversion time for both bus and shunt voltage.

Alert functions are not supported.

Sysfs entries
-------------

======================= =======================================================
in[0-3]_label           Voltage channel labels
in[0-3]_enable          Voltage channel enable controls
in[0-3]_input           Bus voltage (mV) channels
in[4-7]_input           Shunt voltage (mV) for channels 1 to 4 respectively
curr[1-4]_input         Current (mA) measurement channels
power[1-4]_input        Power (uW) measurement channels
energy[1-4]_input       Energy (uJ) accumulated by the chip since its last
                        reset. Overflow of the 32-bit hardware accumulator is
                        not handled.
samples                 Number of samples used in the averaging mode.

                        Supports the list of number of samples:

                          1, 4, 16, 64, 128, 256, 512, 1024

update_interval         Data conversion time in millisecond, following:

                          update_interval = C x S x (BC + SC)

                          * C:	number of enabled channels
                          * S:	number of samples
                          * BC:	bus-voltage conversion time in millisecond
                          * SC:	shunt-voltage conversion time in millisecond

                        Affects both Bus- and Shunt-voltage conversion time.
                        Note that setting update_interval to 0ms sets both BC
                        and SC to 140 us (minimum conversion time).
======================= =======================================================

Chip reset
----------

The chip returns to its power-up defaults after an externally triggered reset,
such as an I2C general call, or a voltage glitch. When a current, power or
energy reading comes back zero, the driver checks whether the chip has lost its
calibration and, if so, restores its configuration. The read then waits up to
100 ms for a fresh conversion and fails with -EAGAIN if none arrives, as with
long averaging settings. The energy accumulators restart from zero after such
a reset. A reset goes unnoticed while only voltages are being read, and until
it is noticed, shunt voltages on channels using the 4x gain read four times
too low.
